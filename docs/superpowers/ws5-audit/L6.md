## ERRORS

### E1 — "C major to Db minor" is not a doubly-chromatic mediant, and does not have zero common tones
- **Location:** `exercisesL6.ts`, exercise `l6u19m3e3`, the `hint` field (lines 359–372).
- **Type:** exercise (hint text)
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** *"In some cases (doubly chromatic mediants like C major to Db minor) there are
  zero common tones, with dramatic chromatic shifts in all voices."*
- **Why it's wrong:** Two independent faults.
  1. **Not a mediant relationship at all.** Db is a minor 2nd above C, not a third. A (chromatic) mediant
     requires roots a major or minor *third* apart. "C → Db" is a second relationship.
  2. **Common-tone count is wrong.** C major = {C, E, G} = pc {0,4,7}; Db minor = {Db, Fb, Ab} = pc {1,4,8}.
     They share pc 4 (E / Fb) — **one** common tone, not zero.
- **Correct version:** Use a real doubly-chromatic mediant that genuinely has zero common tones, e.g.
  **C major → Eb minor** ({3,6,10}, 0 shared) or **C major → Ab minor** ({8,11,3}, 0 shared). Both have
  roots a third from C and share no pitch classes — the textbook definition of a doubly-chromatic
  ("disjunct") mediant.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Kostka–Payne *Tonal Harmony* ch. 24 (chromatic mediants); direct pitch-class
  computation. The (correct) answer choice itself is fine — only the hint's illustrative example is broken.

### E2 — Diminished-7th "Four Possible Resolutions": the fourth-resolution parenthetical is incoherent ("strictly Bbb minor")
- **Location:** `curriculumL6.ts`, module `l6u19m1`, concept "Four Possible Resolutions" (lines 256–262).
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** *"Respell as Ab-Cb-Ebb-Gbb: resolves to A minor (strictly Bbb minor; A minor via
  enharmonic respelling as G#-B-D-F)."*
- **Why it's wrong:** The phrase "strictly Bbb minor" has no theoretical basis. In the spelling
  Ab-Cb-Ebb-Gbb the chord member that acts as leading tone to the target tonic is **Ab (= G#)**, resolving
  up to A. There is no Bbb in this chord and nothing points to a "Bbb minor" tonic. The construction is a
  confused conflation: the *clean* way to reach A minor is to respell the original B-D-F-Ab as **G#-B-D-F**
  (G# = leading tone), exactly as the very next concept ("Applying Dim7 Enharmonic Modulation") states
  correctly. The Ab-Cb-Ebb-Gbb spelling is the "stack-of-minor-thirds" continuation and does not by
  itself yield an A-minor leading-tone reading; pairing it with "(strictly Bbb minor)" is wrong and will
  confuse a careful student.
- **Correct version:** *"...Respell the original chord as G#-B-D-F (G# as leading tone): resolves to A minor.
  The four target tonics — C, Eb, Gb, A — are each a minor third apart."* (Drop the "Bbb minor"
  parenthetical entirely.)
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Aldwell–Schachter ch. 30 / Kostka–Payne ch. 25 (dim7 enharmonic reinterpretation).
  The four target keys themselves (C, Eb, Gb, A = pc 0,3,6,9) and the symmetry claim are correct; only the
  spelling logic for the fourth resolution is garbled.

## QUESTIONABLE — needs human verification

### Q1 — `chord_build` prompts demand a specific inversion the engine cannot validate
- **Location:** `templatesL6.ts`, template `l6u18m1` first block (lines 18–29, "Build a {root}{accidental}
  major triad **in first inversion** (Neapolitan chord)"); compare hand-authored `l6u18m1e1` which
  correctly does *not* ask for inversion.
- **Type:** template
- **Severity:** questionable (engine/pedagogy mismatch, not a pitch error)
- **Concern:** `ChordBuildConfig` has no inversion field, and `validateChordBuild`
  (`validateExercise.ts` lines 194–213) compares **pitch-class sets only** — inversion is invisible to the
  validator. So a learner who builds root-position Db–F–Ab is marked correct even though the prompt said
  "first inversion." The *answer is pitch-correct* (Db major = Db,F,Ab in every key the template samples,
  verified incl. edge key Cb→Cb,Eb,Gb), but the prompt over-specifies relative to what is graded. The
  hint then compounds it ("place the 3rd in the bass") — untestable.
- **Suggested resolution:** Either (a) drop "in first inversion" / "place the 3rd in the bass" from the
  generated prompt+hint so it matches what is graded, or (b) add inversion support to the build validator.
  Flagging rather than asserting a fix because the right call depends on product intent (is inversion meant
  to be assessed at all at this stage?).
- **Confidence:** high (on the mechanism); the "is this a defect or acceptable simplification" judgement is
  the part needing a human.

### Q2 — Common-tone dim7 spelling uses voice-leading spelling, not a stacked-3rds dim7 (defensible but worth a human eye)
- **Location:** `curriculumL6.ts`, module `l6u19m2`, concept "Building CTo7" (C-D#-F#-A) and task
  `l6u19m2t2` (G-A#-C#-E).
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** questionable
- **Concern:** C-D#-F#-A is spelled with letters C, D, F, A — i.e. *not* four consecutive third-letters
  (a "true" stacked dim7 would be C-Eb-Gb-Bbb). This is intentional and standard for a *common-tone* dim7,
  where notes are spelled to show their stepwise resolution (D#→E, F#→G are written as raised leading-tone
  neighbors). Both examples are voice-leading-correct (verified: pc-sets {0,3,6,9} and {1,4,7,10}; common
  tone held, two voices by half step, one by whole step). Kostka–Payne spells CT°7 exactly this way. **No
  change recommended** — flagged only because the file elsewhere is meticulous about stacked-3rds dim7
  spelling (e.g. `l6u19m1e1` uses Bbb), so a reviewer might wrongly "correct" this to match. The
  inconsistency is justified by the different function.
- **Confidence:** medium (high that the spelling is acceptable; medium that the team is aware the two
  dim7 spelling conventions intentionally differ).
- **Source/reasoning:** Kostka–Payne ch. 26 (common-tone diminished seventh).

### Q3 — Augmented-sixth "nationality" labels and the engine's semitone-8 = "Augmented 5th" label
- **Location:** Convention note spanning `l6u18m2`/`l6u18m3` text & exercises; and engine
  `chords.ts` `INTERVAL_LABELS[8] = 'Augmented 5th'` (line 423–424) used by `validateExercise`.
- **Type:** explanation (convention)
- **Severity:** questionable / nitpick
- **Concern:** (a) The It/Fr/Ger "national" naming and the b6–1–(b3/2)–#4 spellings used throughout match
  the dominant English-language convention (Kostka–Payne, Laitz). No error. (b) Separately, the shared
  engine labels pitch-class 8 as "Augmented 5th"; in an augmented-sixth context the b6 scale degree (e.g.
  Ab in C) is a *minor 6th* above the tonic, not an augmented 5th. This never surfaces as a wrong **answer
  key** in L6 (the aug-6 chords are taught in prose; no `interval_id` exercise targets pc 8 here), so it is
  out of strict L6 scope, but it would mislabel intervals if an aug-6-related interval exercise were ever
  added. Flagged for awareness; no L6 content change required.
- **Confidence:** high (that current L6 content is unaffected); medium (that it's worth fixing engine-side).

### N2 — `l6u19m3` (curriculum) folds chromatic mediants, altered dominants, AND the omnibus into one module while the matching template `l6u19m3` is titled "Omnibus Progression" only
- **Location:** `curriculumL6.ts` `l6u19m3` vs `templatesL6.ts` `l6u19m3`. The template's MC items
  (omnibus contrary-chromatic motion, dominant prolongation) are all theory-correct; the title mismatch is

  `l6u19m3` but the templates put the chromatic-mediant MC items under template `l6u19m4`. Content is
  correct in both; the module-number-to-topic mapping is inconsistent between the two files.
- **Type:** template — **Severity:** nitpick — **Confidence:** high.

### N3 — "five positions around the circle of fifths in one step" (Neapolitan/dim7 modules) describes the C→Db relationship loosely
- **Location:** `curriculumL6.ts` `l6u18m4` task `l6u18m4t3` ("five positions around the circle of fifths
  in one step") and milestone prose.
- **Type:** explanation — **Severity:** nitpick
- **Concern:** C→Db is 5 steps one way / 7 the other on the 12-position circle (it is the maximally
  distant *flat-side* neighbour, a half step / 5 sharps-or-7-flats apart). "Five positions" is defensible
  for the flat direction but reads as if Db were uniformly "5 away"; the more standard framing is "a half
  step apart, near-maximally distant on the circle." Minor wording; the pedagogical point (very distant
  keys) is sound and the enharmonic mechanism is correct.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## Coverage & limitations

- **What was verified by computation:** every `chord_build`/`scale_build` answer key, every enharmonic
  respelling asserted in prose or tasks, dim7 resolution target sets, chromatic-mediant common-tone counts,
  octatonic interval inventory, whole-tone/dim7/aug-triad transposition counts, and all invertible-
  counterpoint interval/permutation arithmetic. These reproduce the engine's own `buildChord` spelling
  logic (`chords.ts`) and pitch-class math (`notes.ts`), so they reflect exactly what the app will grade.
- **What was verified against textbook canon (no web search needed):** Neapolitan function/usage/voice
  leading; aug-6 family identity, function, and outward resolution; Ger+6 ~= dom7 and its modulatory use;
  dim7 four-way symmetry; common-tone dim7; chromatic mediants; species counterpoint rules; suspension
  taxonomy; invertible/triple counterpoint; figured bass; transposing-instrument intervals. All consistent
  with Kostka–Payne, Aldwell–Schachter, and Laitz.
- **Convention-dependent points checked and found standard:** the It/Fr/Ger "national" labels and their
  b6–1–(2/b3)–#4 spellings; doubling scale-degree 1 in It+6; the cadential-6/4 remedy for Gr+6 parallel
  fifths; lowercase/°/+ Roman-numeral conventions. Where a reputable alternative exists (e.g., some texts
  discuss It+6 doubling differently, or use "lowered submediant" vs "minor sixth" naming), the file's
  choices fall within accepted practice and were **not** flagged as errors.
- **Not assessed (out of scope or not present):** audio rendering of the `tryThisQuery` strings (e.g.
  whether the synth actually voices "Db/F" or "Ab7" as described); the PT/ES translation overlays
  (`i18n/content/*L6*`); whether queries like `Bdim7`, `C7#5`, `whole tone scale`, `C diminished scale`
  resolve in the query layer (the chord-build path was verified; the free-text query parser was not
  exercised). The aug-6 chords have **no** dedicated build/quality in the engine (`CHORD_FORMULAS` has no
  augmented-sixth entry), so they are taught and tested entirely in prose/MC — correctly — but cannot be
  machine-built or machine-graded as such.
- **Residual risk:** low. The only items that could change a learner's score are the inversion-prompt
  mismatch (Q1, answer still pitch-correct) — nothing in the answer *keys* is wrong. The two ERRORS are
  confined to explanatory/hint prose and would mislead a careful reader without causing an incorrect grade.
