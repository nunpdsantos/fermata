## ERRORS

### E1 — "Andalusian cadence" is mislabelled (it is a different progression)
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u23m1` ("Pop Progressions and Nashville Numbers"), concept "Common Pop Progressions" (~line 644).
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** "The i-bVI-bIII-bVII 'Andalusian' cadence sounds dark and cinematic."
- **Why it's wrong:** The Andalusian cadence is **i–bVII–bVI–V** — a descending tetrachord bass from the minor tonic down to the **dominant (V)**, the defining flamenco progression. The loop the text names "Andalusian" (i-bVI-bIII-bVII) contains no V chord and ends on bVII, not V. i-bVI-bIII-bVII is in fact the minor-key rotation of the I-V-vi-IV "Axis" loop (start the Axis on its vi and you get i-bVI-bIII-bVII), which the same module elsewhere discusses. The label is simply attached to the wrong four chords.
- **Correct version:** Either rename to the actual Andalusian cadence **i-bVII-bVI-V**, or keep i-bVI-bIII-bVII and relabel it (e.g. "minor Axis / vi-rotation of I-V-vi-IV"). Do not call i-bVI-bIII-bVII "Andalusian."
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Wikipedia "Andalusian cadence" (i–bVII–bVI–V); StudyBass, Chordly corroborate the descending-to-V definition.

### E2 — Chromatic turnaround: bass-motion description is self-contradictory and factually wrong
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u21m6` ("Rhythm Changes and Turnarounds"), task `l7u21m6t3` (line 333).
- **Type:** explanation (task instruction)
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** Chords Cmaj7–Eb7–Ab7–Db7, then: "The bass descends chromatically by minor thirds and fourths: C–Eb–Ab–Db."
- **Why it's wrong:** (a) "Descends" is wrong — C→Eb→Ab→Db ascends (C→Eb is up a m3; Eb→Ab and Ab→Db are up P4s). (b) "Chromatically" is wrong and self-contradictory — chromatic motion means by half-step; minor-thirds-and-fourths motion is by definition not chromatic. The bass line C-Eb-Ab-Db is neither chromatic nor descending. (The tritone-sub identifications in the same sentence — Eb7 for A7, Ab7 for D7, Db7 for G7 — are all correct; only the bass-motion gloss is wrong.)
- **Correct version:** Drop "descends chromatically." State the actual motion: roots C-Eb-Ab-Db move by ascending minor 3rd then two ascending perfect 4ths (equivalently descending major 6th / descending 5ths). If a *chromatic* bass is wanted, that is the I-vi-ii-V chromatic-approach turnaround, not this tritone-sub turnaround.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Direct pitch-class arithmetic on the stated roots; internal contradiction within the sentence.

### E3 — `C7alt` taught content vs. bundled chord engine: only 2 of 4 alterations are rendered
- **Location:** Pervasive. Teaching text in `l7u21m1` (concept "Alterations", line 39), `l7u21m2` (concept "The Alt Chord", line 87; task `l7u21m2t2`, line 108), `l7u23m3` (concept "The Altered Scale", line 768; task `l7u23m3e3`), `l7u23m5` (task `l7u23m5t3`), and milestone `tryThisPrompt` (line 971). Exercise hint `l7u21m1e3` (line 56). All route `tryThisQuery: 'C7alt'` / typed `C7alt` to the engine's `dominant7alt`.
- **Type:** explanation / exercise (consistency between taught content and the tool the tasks instruct students to use)
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** The text repeatedly (and correctly, per canon) defines the alt chord as a dominant 7th with **all four** alterations: "b9, #9, #11 (b5), and b13 (#5) … every non-shell tone is chromatically displaced" (l7u21m2). Tasks instruct the learner to type `C7alt` and observe this.
- **Why it's a problem:** The bundled engine defines `dominant7alt: [0, 4, 6, 10, 13]` = R, 3, **b5, b7, b9** only (`chords.ts` line 82). That is just **two** alterations (b5 and b9); it omits #9 and #5/b13. So `C7alt` renders C-E-Gb-Bb-Db, which directly contradicts "every extension altered." Under standard jazz canon the alt chord/altered scale carry all four alterations (b9, #9, b5/#11, #5/b13). The text is canonically correct; the tool the text tells students to use is not, so the lesson is internally inconsistent at the exact moment of the "Try This."
- **Correct version:** This is an engine-definition limitation (core file, out of edit scope here). Flag for the engine owner: either expand `dominant7alt` toward the canonical altered content (commonly voiced R-3-b7 + b9-#9-#11-b13, often rootless/5th-omitted) or adjust the L7 text so it does not promise alterations the rendered chord lacks. The `tryThisQuery: 'C7alt'` cues and hints will otherwise mislead.
- **Confidence:** high (the contradiction is verified directly from the two files; the canon is well established)
- **Source/reasoning:** `chords.ts` `dominant7alt` formula vs. L7 text; Wikipedia "Altered scale" and Jazzadvice confirm the alt chord carries b9/#9/b5/#5.

## QUESTIONABLE (convention-dependent or muddled derivation — needs human verification)

### Q1 — Back-door dominant explained as "tritone sub of E7, the V7/vi"
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u21m4`, concept "The Back-Door Dominant" (line 204).
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** questionable
- **What it says now:** "the back-door dominant is a relative of tritone substitution — Bb7 is the tritone sub of E7, the V7/vi."
- **The concern:** Arithmetically Bb is a tritone from E, and E7 is V7/vi in C, so the literal statement is not false. But it is a non-standard and misleading *derivation*: the tritone sub of E7 shares E7's guide tones (G#/D#) and naturally resolves toward A (vi), not C. The back-door dominant bVII7 (Bb7→C) is standardly explained as borrowed from the parallel minor / the dominant of bIII (Eb), sharing guide tones with an **altered G7 (the V)** — not with E7. Presenting E7 as the source obscures why Bb7 resolves to C.
- **Suggested framing (verify):** Derive bVII7 as a parallel-minor borrowing / iv-related sound resolving by guide tones (Bb7's 3rd D→E and 7th Ab→G into Cmaj7), and note its kinship to the V chord, rather than to E7.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Source/reasoning:** Wikipedia "Backdoor progression"; Learn Jazz Standards; jazzguitar.be — all relate bVII7 to V/parallel-minor, not to V7/vi.

### Q2 — "9sus4 / 7sus4 built on the 5th of a minor chord creates a Dorian flavor"
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u21m2`, concept "Suspended Dominants: 7sus4" (line 94).
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** questionable
- **What it says now:** "The 7sus4 also serves as a Dorian sound (the sus4 voicing built on the 5th of a minor chord creates a Dorian flavor)."
- **The concern:** The usual statement is that a **dom7sus / 9sus chord built on the 5th of a ii-chord (i.e., V9sus) substitutes for the ii** (e.g., G9sus ≈ Dm7/G), giving a modal/Dorian color over the ii-V. "Built on the 5th of a minor chord" is a loose, potentially backwards phrasing (G is the V-related root of Dm, not a note "of" a D-minor triad). Defensible as shorthand, but imprecise enough to confuse.
- **Suggested framing (verify):** "A 9sus chord a 4th below a minor chord (the V-of-ii position), e.g. G9sus over Dm, yields a Dorian/modal color and often substitutes for the ii."
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Source/reasoning:** Standard jazz treatment of sus/9sus as ii-substitute; the present wording is internally shaky ("5th of a minor chord").

### Q3 — `dominant7alt` answer key calls it "b5/#5 and b9/#9" while engine renders only b5+b9
- **Location:** `exercisesL7.ts`, exercise `l7u21m1e3` (correct choice + hint, lines 50–56).
- **Type:** exercise
- **Severity:** questionable (the *answer text* is canonically correct; flagged because it is the same engine mismatch as E3 and a student verifying with the tool will see a contradiction)

- **The concern:** As prose, this is the right definition. The issue is consistency with the engine (see E3): the bundled `C7alt` cannot demonstrate #9 or #5. Not an error in the answer key itself.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Source/reasoning:** See E3.

### Q4 — Lydian/Mixolydian modal "chord progression" recipes are illustrative, not canonical
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u22m4`, concept "Modal Chord Progressions" (line 541): "Lydian gravitates to I, II, and vii"; "Dorian favors i, II, and IV."
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** questionable
- **What it says now:** Assigns characteristic chords per mode; for Lydian lists "I, II, and vii" as the gravitational chords, with "the raised 4th appears in both II and vii."
- **The concern:** The genuinely *characteristic* Lydian motion is **I–II** (the major II built on scale-degree 2 contains the #4); "vii" (a minor triad on the leading tone, vii in C Lydian = B minor) does contain the #4 (F#) but is far less idiomatic/standard as a "Lydian gravity" chord than I–II. Dorian "favors i, II, IV" — the canonical Dorian signature chord is **IV (major)**, which holds the natural 6; listing "II" (which in Dorian is minor, ii, and does NOT contain the characteristic 6) is debatable. These are pedagogical simplifications; defensible but not uniformly endorsed.
- **Suggested check:** Emphasise I–II for Lydian and i–IV (major-chord-on-natural-6) for Dorian as the primary modal signatures; treat the others as secondary.
- **Confidence:** low (convention-dependent; not wrong, just non-standard emphasis)
- **Source/reasoning:** Common modal-harmony pedagogy (characteristic chord = the chord containing the characteristic note: Dorian IV, Lydian II, Mixolydian bVII, Phrygian bII).

## NITPICKS

### N1 — `Dm11` as "a quartal stack in chord form"; `D5` as "quintal"
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u22m5`, tasks `l7u22m5t1`/`t2` and concepts "Quartal Voicings" / "Quintal Voicings"; exercise `l7u22m5e1`.
- **Type:** explanation/exercise
- **Severity:** nitpick
- **Concern:** Dm11 (`minor11` = [0,3,7,10,14,17] = D-F-A-C-E-G) is a *tertian* chord; its notes can be *re-voiced* in fourths (D-G-C-F), but as the engine builds it, it includes the 3rd (F) and 5th (A). Saying typing `Dm11` lets you "hear a quartal stack" overstates it; the quartal sonority requires the specific fourths voicing, which `chord_build` does not enforce (it checks pitch classes, not voicing). Likewise `D5` (root+5th) is a single fifth, fine as "simplest fifth stack" but not really "quintal harmony." Pedagogically acceptable shorthand; flagged as imprecise.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Source/reasoning:** Engine `CHORD_FORMULAS.minor11` includes 3rd and 5th; quartal vs. tertian is a voicing distinction the exercise type cannot verify.

### N2 — Fixed semitone→interval labels can't show #5/b13 enharmonic spelling nuance
- **Location:** `chords.ts` shared labels (INTERVAL_LABELS 8 = "Augmented 5th"; SHORT 8 = "#5", 20 = "b13") — surfaces in L7 alt-chord displays.
- **Type:** explanation (label, shared core)
- **Severity:** nitpick
- **Concern:** Not an L7-content error; noted only because L7 leans heavily on #5/b13 enharmonic equivalence and the engine's fixed semitone→label map will always show one spelling. No action needed inside L7 content.
- **Confidence:** high (it is merely a display convention)
- **Source/reasoning:** `chords.ts` lines 425/465.

### N3 — "Hindu scale" as an alias for Mixolydian b6
- **Location:** `curriculumL7.ts`, module `l7u23m3`, concept "Lydian Dominant and Mixolydian b6" (line 761): "Mixolydian b6 (also called Hindu or Aeolian dominant)."
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** nitpick
- **Concern:** "Hindu scale" and "Aeolian dominant" are both in circulation as names for Mixolydian b6, so the aliases are defensible. The "Hindu" label is informal/dated and varies by source; fine to keep but it is the least standardized of the three. No correction required.
- **Confidence:** low
- **Source/reasoning:** Mixed usage across jazz pedagogy; the engine itself comments `mixolydian_b6 … (Aeolian Dominant)` (`scales.ts` line 40).
