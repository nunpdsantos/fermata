## ERRORS

### E1 — Wrong interval label in worked SATB voicing
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts`, module `l3u10m1`, task `l3u10m1t1` (line ~286), type **task/explanation**, severity **error**.
- **Says now:** "write a C major triad in SATB: Bass C2, Tenor G3, Alto E4, Soprano C5. Verify all spacing rules: soprano-alto gap (a minor 6th — under an octave), **alto-tenor gap (a major 3rd — under an octave)**, tenor-bass gap (a perfect 5th). All valid."
- **Why it's wrong:** The alto-tenor gap is E4 down to G3 = G3→E4 = **9 semitones = a major SIXTH**, not a major 3rd. (A major 3rd is 4 semitones; G3→B3 would be a 3rd, but the alto is E4.) Soprano-alto (E4→C5 = 8 st = minor 6th) is correctly labeled. Tenor-bass is C2→G3 = 19 semitones = a **perfect 12th** (compound P5), not literally "a perfect 5th" — see N1.
- **Correct version:** alto-tenor gap is **a major 6th**. (The voicing itself is otherwise valid: doubling = C/G/E/C → root doubled in root position, correct; tenor–bass exceeding an octave is explicitly permitted by the concept text above it.)
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Source/reasoning:** Semitone arithmetic; cross-checked against `intervals.ts` (`'major 6th': 9`, `'minor 6th': 8`, `'major 3rd': 4`). MIDI: C2=36, G3=55, E4=64, C5=72.

### E2 — Minor-key deceptive cadence: wrong shared-tone count + unfounded conclusion
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts`, module `l3u11m1`, concept "Half, Plagal, and Deceptive Cadences" (line ~505), type **explanation**, severity **error**.
- **Says now:** "In minor keys, the deceptive cadence is V to VI — a major chord (e.g., E7 to F in A minor). Because VI is major rather than minor, the shared-tone logic differs: **VI shares only one tone with i (the tonic note itself)**, giving the minor deceptive cadence a warmer, more dramatic surprise than its major-key counterpart."
- **Why it's wrong:** In A minor, i = A–C–E and VI = F–A–C. They share **A and C — two common tones**, not one. (1̂ and 3̂ of the tonic are both present in the submediant, in minor exactly as in major.) The claim of "only one tone (the tonic note itself)" is factually false, and the causal conclusion that follows ("shared-tone logic differs… warmer, more dramatic surprise") is built entirely on that false premise. The standard explanation is the opposite: VI works as a tonic substitute *because* it shares 1̂ and 3̂.
- **Correct version:** "VI shares two tones with i — the tonic (1̂) and the mediant (3̂); in A minor, F major shares A and C with A minor. As in major keys, this two-tone overlap is exactly why the submediant can substitute for the expected tonic." Drop the "warmer/more dramatic because the logic differs" claim, or re-ground it (the genuine minor-specific point is the voice-leading constraint — to avoid the ↑6̂–↓7̂ augmented-2nd problem, the 3rd of VI is typically doubled — not a different shared-tone count).
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Source/reasoning:** Triad spellings; corroborated by the FSU/N. Rogers "The Submediant Chord (vi or VI)" handout — "the submediant chord shares the tonic triad's two most important tones (1̂ and 3̂)" — which applies to both major vi and minor VI. https://myweb.fsu.edu/nrogers/Handouts/vi.pdf

### E3 — SATB ranges stated inconsistently across files (factual conflict)
- **Location:** three files, type **explanation**, severity **error** (at least one variant is non-canonical, and they contradict each other):
  - `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u10m1` concept (line ~263): Soprano C4–G5, **Alto F3–C5**, Tenor C3–G4, **Bass E2–C4**.
  - `templatesL3.ts` `l3u10m1` hintTemplate (line ~174): "Soprano C4-G5, **Alto F3-D5**, Tenor C3-G4, **Bass E2-D4**."
  - `exercisesL3.ts` `l3u10m1e1` (line ~319): Soprano C4–G5 (consistent with the others on soprano only).
- **Why it's wrong/the concern:** The alto top and bass top differ between the concept (C5 / C4) and the template hint (D5 / D4) for the *same* module. At most one can be the intended convention; presented as fact, they conflict. (Textbook SATB ranges vary by source, but a single curriculum should not give two different numbers for the same voices in the same module.)
- **Correct version:** Pick one convention and use it in all three places. A common textbook set (e.g., Laitz / Clendinning) is roughly Bass E2–C4, Tenor C3–A4 (or G4), Alto G3–D5 (or F3–C5), Soprano C4–G5 — the specific bounds matter less than internal consistency. Recommend reconciling concept and template to identical figures.
- **Confidence:** high (that they are inconsistent); medium (on which single set is "most authoritative" — sources legitimately differ, so this is an internal-consistency error, not a claim that one specific octave bound is the only correct one).
- **Source/reasoning:** Direct comparison of the three strings.

## QUESTIONABLE (needs human verification — not asserting a fix)

### Q1 — Augmented-Major-7 chord called "III+maj7" with augmented triad: spelling vs. naming
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u9m4` concepts (lines ~198, ~206); template `l3u9m4` (`augmented_major7` paired with root C, line ~133).
- **Concern:** The content names the III chord of A harmonic minor "III+maj7" = C–E–G♯–B and calls it "an augmented triad with a major seventh." This is theoretically correct (`CHORD_FORMULAS.augmented_major7 = [0,4,8,11]`, verified). Flagging only because some textbooks treat III+ as a non-functional sonority or spell its fifth differently in certain voice-leading contexts; the app's spelling G♯ (from the raised 7̂) is the standard and defensible choice. **No error** — recorded only to surface the naming-convention sensitivity.
- **Confidence:** high that the app's spelling/quality is correct; low that any change is warranted.
- **Source/reasoning:** Built `buildChord` by hand for all 7 degrees of A harmonic minor; all match `getDiatonicSeventhChordsHarmonicMinor`.

### Q2 — Cadential 6/4 always on a "strong beat"
- **Location:** `exercisesL3.ts` `l3u10m4e2` (line ~491): correct answer "On a strong beat, immediately before the dominant (V) chord"; `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u10m4` concept (line ~445).
- **Concern:** "Metrically accented" placement is the textbook norm and the keyed answer is the best of the four offered. Strictly, the cadential 6/4 is accented *relative to its resolution* but does not always fall on a globally "strong beat." Distractors are clearly worse, so the answer is fine; flagging the absolutism, not an error.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### Q3 — Half cadence "any chord moving to the dominant"
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u11m1` (line ~505) and `exercisesL3.ts` `l3u11m1e2` (line ~547): "Any cadence that ends on the dominant (V) chord."
- **Concern:** Correct and standard shorthand; the exercise hint correctly lists typical predominants. Logged only because "any chord → V" can read as over-broad. **Not an error.**
- **Confidence:** high (it's correct).

### Q4 — "Law of the shortest way" as a named rule
- **Location:** `exercisesL3.ts` `l3u10m3e3` (line ~451): keyed-correct "Each voice should move to the nearest available chord tone, preferring stepwise motion."
- **Concern:** The answer's *content* (minimal motion / nearest chord tone) is correct and the best option offered. The phrase "law of the shortest way" (Gesetz des nächsten Weges) is a real but informal label some curricula don't use. The exercise is sound; flagged only for convention-dependent terminology.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## NITPICKS

### N1 — "Perfect 5th" for a compound (12th) interval
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u10m1t1` (same line as E1): "tenor-bass gap (a perfect 5th)."
- **Concern:** C2→G3 is a perfect **12th** (octave + P5). "A perfect 5th" describes the class mod octave but is literally inaccurate for the specified octaves. Fold into the E1 fix; tenor–bass spacing > an octave is allowed.
- **Confidence:** high.

### N2 — "Eb instruments … sound a major sixth lower (or equivalently, a minor third higher)"
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u11m5` concept (line ~737).
- **Concern:** Correct for the **alto saxophone** it names (sounds a M6 below written). Loose if read as a blanket rule for all "Eb instruments" (Eb sopranino sounds a m3 higher; Eb baritone an octave + M6 lower). Accurate as written for the example.
- **Confidence:** high (alto sax); medium (class-level looseness).

### N3 — "Only three distinct diminished seventh chords in the entire chromatic scale"
- **Location:** `curriculumL3.ts` `l3u9m1` task `l3u9m1t3` (line ~60).
- **Concern:** Correct (exactly 3 distinct fully-diminished-7th sonorities by pitch-class content). Phrasing slightly loose but true. Logged as verified.
- **Confidence:** high.

### N4 — Anticipation / "all NCTs … resolve to consonance"
- **Location:** `exercisesL3.ts` `l3u11m4e3` hint (line ~741); `templatesL3.ts` `l3u11m5` choiceSet 4 (line ~510).
- **Concern:** An anticipation does not "resolve" in the prepare→dissonance→resolve sense — it is absorbed when the harmony arrives. The template's blanket "all non-chord tones create dissonance that resolves to consonance" is over-general for anticipations and pedal points, but it is the keyed-correct option against clearly-false distractors, so the exercise still functions.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## Coverage & limitations

- **Fully covered:** every concept explanation, objective, task, milestone in all 13 L3 modules; all 45 hand-authored exercises; all 14 templates and the generator logic that turns them into answer keys (traced through `exerciseGenerator.ts` + `validateExercise.ts`).
- **chord_build correctness method:** because `validateChordBuild` accepts any enharmonic spelling matching the pitch-class set of `buildChord(root, quality)`, chord_build answer keys cannot be "wrong-noted" by the data; the audit therefore checked that each prompt *names* the chord that root+quality builds, and that index-paired templates pair correctly. Learner-facing enharmonic spelling is governed by `CHORD_LETTER_DISTANCES` (e.g., dim7 7th at letter-distance 6 → B♭♭ not A for Cdim7), which is correct.
- **Not in scope / not assessed:** pedagogical ordering, prose style, audio playback, i18n (PT/ES) overlays, and whether `tryThisQuery` strings resolve in the query engine (only the correctness of the theory text was audited).
- **Convention-dependent points** were checked against standard common-practice sources and web-verified where contested (IAC subtypes; minor deceptive shared tones). Where reputable sources legitimately differ (SATB exact range bounds, "law of the shortest way" terminology, cadential-6/4 "strong beat" absolutism), the finding is marked questionable/nitpick rather than asserted as a definitive fix.
- **No fabricated corrections:** the two asserted prose ERRORs (E1 interval label, E2 shared-tone count) are pure arithmetic / triad-spelling facts; E3 is an internal contradiction. Anything convention-sensitive is in QUESTIONABLE/NITPICK.
