# Fermata — Level 4 (Diatonic Mastery) Music-Theory Correctness Audit

**Scope:** Level 4 curriculum content (Units 12–14).
**Files audited:**
- `src/core/constants/curriculumL4.ts` — 15 modules of teaching text
- `src/data/exercises/exercisesL4.ts` — 45 hand-authored exercises
- `src/data/exercises/templatesL4.ts` — 15 module template configs (~80 generated exercises)
- Cross-referenced PT/ES overlays (`src/i18n/content/{pt,es}/templatesL4.ts`), `chords.ts`, `intervals.ts`, `scales.ts`, `glossary.ts`, `progressionPatterns.ts`, `exerciseGenerator.ts`, `exerciseTemplates.ts`.

**Reviewed:** 15 modules / 45 hand-authored exercises / 15 template configs.

**Finding counts by severity:**
- **Errors: 3**
- **Questionable: 2**
- **Nitpicks: 3**

**Conventions checked against:** Kostka & Payne *Tonal Harmony*; Laitz *The Complete Musician*; Fux *Gradus ad Parnassum* / Open Music Theory for species counterpoint; standard common-practice Roman-numeral / functional theory. Where a point is convention-dependent it is flagged and the convention named.

**Headline:** The hand-authored explanatory text and exercise answer keys are, on the whole, accurate and well-constructed — V7 construction/resolution, tendency-tone behavior, inversion resolutions, diatonic-seventh qualities, functional categories, and sequence types are all correct. The serious problems are concentrated in `templatesL4.ts`: (1) a factually inverted statement about the 2-3 bass suspension that is propagated verbatim into PT and ES, and (2) three template configs whose counterpoint content is attached to modules about entirely different topics.

## ERRORS

### E1. 2-3 bass suspension stated to resolve UPWARD (it resolves downward)
- **Location:** `src/data/exercises/templatesL4.ts`, module `l4u12m1`, `hintTemplate` (line ~21): "…The bass suspension 2-3 resolves upward. Chain suspensions create sequences."
- **Type:** template
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:** "The bass suspension 2-3 resolves upward."
- **Why it's wrong:** The 2-3 suspension is the bass-voice suspension, and like every standard suspension it **resolves DOWNWARD by step**. Its "2-3" label describes the interval *above the bass*: while the suspended bass note is held, an upper voice forms a 2nd against it; when the **bass descends by step**, that interval opens to a 3rd. The bass moving down is the entire mechanism. The app's own curriculum text states this correctly — `curriculumL4.ts` `l4u12m1` objective 3 and concept "The 2-3 Bass Suspension" say "the bass resolves downward by step" and "as the bass descends, that interval grows from a 2nd to a 3rd" (citing Kostka/Payne Ch. 12). The template directly contradicts the curriculum it accompanies.
- **Correct version:** "The bass suspension 2-3 resolves **downward** (the bass descends by step; the interval above the bass grows from a 2nd to a 3rd)."
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Kostka & Payne, *Tonal Harmony*, ch. on suspensions; corroborated by My Music Theory, learnmusictheory.net, and Essays on Music ("suspensions in the bass"). All suspensions resolve down by step; the 2-3 is the bass form. The literal intervals may appear as 10-9 but are labeled 2-3.
- **Propagation note (systemic):** the same error is translated into both localized overlays:
  - `src/i18n/content/pt/templatesL4.ts` line 20: "A suspensão de baixo 2-3 resolve para cima."
  - `src/i18n/content/es/templatesL4.ts` line 20: "El retardo de bajo 2-3 resuelve hacia arriba."
  Fixing the English source requires re-deriving both overlays.

### E2. Counterpoint template configs mapped to non-counterpoint modules (topic mismatch)
- **Location:** `src/data/exercises/templatesL4.ts`, modules `l4u14m3`, `l4u14m4`, `l4u14m5`.
- **Type:** template
- **Severity:** error
- **What it says now:**
  - `l4u14m3` generates **third-species counterpoint** questions.
  - `l4u14m4` generates **fourth-species counterpoint / suspension** questions.
  - `l4u14m5` generates **fifth-species (free) counterpoint** questions.
- **Why it's wrong:** Those module IDs are NOT counterpoint modules. Per `curriculumL4.ts`:
  - `l4u14m3` = **"Chromatic Embellishment"** (chromatic passing/neighbor tones)
  - `l4u14m4` = **"Roman Numeral Analysis Practice"**
  - `l4u14m5` = **"Minor Key Harmony in Detail"**
  Unit 14's only counterpoint module is `l4u14m1` ("First and Second Species Counterpoint"). The generator (`exerciseGenerator.ts` `generateExercises`) keys output strictly by `moduleId`, so a learner studying Chromatic Embellishment will be served third-species counterpoint questions, etc. The generated exercises are topically disconnected from the modules' concepts/objectives. (The counterpoint statements inside the templates are themselves mostly accurate — see E-note below — but they are attached to the wrong subject.)
- **Correct version:** Re-target these template configs to their actual module topics (chromatic NCTs, Roman-numeral analysis, minor-key harmony), or, if third/fourth/fifth species are intended as curriculum, add the corresponding modules so IDs line up. As written, the topic-to-module mapping is incorrect.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Source/reasoning:** Direct comparison of `moduleId` values in `templatesL4.ts` against module titles/objectives/concepts in `curriculumL4.ts`. The mismatch is unambiguous.

### E3. Cut time described as containing "the same number of quarter notes as 4/4"
- **Location:** `src/data/exercises/exercisesL4.ts`, `l4u14m2e3`, hint: "Cut time (alla breve) is 2/2… While it contains the same number of quarter notes as 4/4…"; mirrored in `curriculumL4.ts` `l4u14m2` concept "Mixed Meters and Cut Time": "the same written notes as 4/4 but felt in 2."
- **Type:** exercise (hint) + explanation
- **Severity:** error (low-impact, but a true factual slip)
- **What it says now:** 2/2 "contains the same number of quarter notes as 4/4."
- **Why it's wrong / the concern:** A measure of 2/2 and a measure of 4/4 both span **four quarter-note durations** — so by *duration* this is fine. But the sentence implies a contrast where there is none, and the surrounding claim that cut time gives "a faster tempo feel at the same tempo" is self-contradictory: tempo is unchanged, only the *felt* beat unit (half note vs quarter) and conducting pattern change. The pedagogically correct point is that 2/2 reinterprets the **beat unit** (half-note beat, 2 beats/bar) versus 4/4 (quarter-note beat, 4 beats/bar) with identical total bar duration. The exercise hint's "same number of quarter notes as 4/4… faster-moving feel at the same tempo" muddles duration, beat count, and tempo.
- **Correct version:** "Cut time (alla breve) is 2/2: two half-note beats per measure. A 2/2 bar has the same total duration as a 4/4 bar, but it is counted in 2 (half-note beat) rather than 4 (quarter-note beat), giving a broader, faster-moving feel."
- **Confidence:** medium (the statement is defensible as written about *durations*; flagged because the phrasing conflates beat-count, note-count, and tempo and can mislead).

> **E2 sub-note (informational, not a separate finding):** Within the mis-targeted counterpoint templates, the theory itself is sound: third-species cambiata "steps away then leaps in the same direction" (`l4u14m3`) correctly describes the classic descending nota cambiata (down a step to the dissonance, then down a third); fourth-species "7-6 most common upper-voice suspension" and "2-3 is NOT the most common upper-voice suspension" (`l4u14m4`) are correct (the 2-3 is a *bass* suspension and cannot occur in upper voices); fifth-species "freely combines all four previous species" and the melodic-climax rule (`l4u14m5`) are correct. The error is placement (E2), not content.

## QUESTIONABLE (needs human verification)

### Q1. Second-species dissonance described as "passing tones OR neighbor tones"
- **Location:** `src/core/constants/curriculumL4.ts`, `l4u14m1`, concept "First and Second Species": "…weak beats may be dissonant if they are passing tones or neighbor tones approached and left by step."
- **Type:** explanation
- **Severity:** questionable
- **What it says now:** Second-species weak-beat dissonance may be a passing tone **or a neighbor tone**.
- **The concern:** Under **strict Fuxian species counterpoint** — which this module invokes by name — the *only* permitted dissonance in second species is the **passing tone** (entered by step and continued by step *in the same direction*). A **dissonant neighbor tone is generally deferred to third species**; in second species a neighbor may appear only if it is *consonant*. Notably, the same module's own exercise `l4u14m1e3` gets the strict rule right: "Dissonances may occur on weak beats only, approached and left by step **in the same direction**" (= passing tone, correct=true). So the curriculum prose is looser than both Fux and its own exercise. Some modern textbooks do permit dissonant neighbors earlier, so this is convention-dependent rather than flatly wrong.
- **Suggested version (if strict species intended):** "…weak beats may be dissonant **only as passing tones** (approached and left by step in the same direction). Dissonant neighbor tones are introduced in third species."
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Source/reasoning:** Fux, *Gradus ad Parnassum*; Open Music Theory "Second-Species Counterpoint" ("the only dissonance permitted in the second species" is the passing tone; neighbors in 2nd species must be consonant). Flagged questionable because pedagogies legitimately differ on when the dissonant neighbor is admitted.

### Q2. interval_id template asks to "identify the interval between two voices" but grades only the named interval, with a consonance/dissonance hint that doesn't match the task
- **Location:** `src/data/exercises/templatesL4.ts`, `l4u14m2`, second template (`type: 'interval_id'`): prompt "What is the interval formed between the two voices? Starting from {root}.", `intervals: [3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12]`, hint about consonance vs dissonance treatment.
- **Type:** template
- **Severity:** questionable
- **What it says now / concern:** The `interval_id` exercise type (per `exercise.ts` and `exerciseGenerator.ts`) simply asks the learner to name the interval `targetSemitones` above the root; the engine computes the answer, so **no generated answer key can be wrong here**. However: (a) the prompt frames a two-voice counterpoint judgment that the exercise does not actually test; (b) the hint asserts "consonances are 3rds, 6ths, and perfect intervals (P1, P5, P8)," yet the `intervals` pool includes **5 = Perfect 4th**, which species counterpoint treats as a **dissonance** in two-voice texture (the same level's `l4u14m1e2` correctly says so). A learner reading the hint and then meeting a P4 receives mixed messages. No incorrect answer is produced, but the framing is internally inconsistent.
- **Suggested fix:** Either drop the consonance/dissonance framing from this interval-naming template, or remove `5` (P4) / split the pool so the consonance claim in the hint holds for every generated value.
- **Confidence:** high (that no answer is mis-keyed); medium (that the mismatch warrants a change).

## NITPICKS

### N1. "Augmented 5th" label used for 8-semitone interval (shared constant)
- **Location:** `src/core/constants/chords.ts` `INTERVAL_LABELS[8] = 'Augmented 5th'` (used app-wide, surfaces in L4 chord displays).
- **Type:** explanation (shared constant)
- **Severity:** nitpick
- **Note:** 8 semitones is enharmonically both a minor 6th and an augmented 5th; labeling it "Augmented 5th" is fine in augmented-chord contexts but is a fixed global label. Not an L4-specific error; noted only because L4 augmented-triad material (`l4u14m5` III+) relies on it. No change required for L4 correctness.
- **Confidence:** high

### N2. Escape tone described as "typically accented" in a template, but escape tones are unaccented
- **Location:** `src/data/exercises/templatesL4.ts`, `l4u12m2`, `hintTemplate`: "Appoggiatura: leap to dissonance, step to resolution. Escape tone: step to dissonance, leap away. **Both are typically accented.**"
- **Type:** template
- **Severity:** nitpick (hint only; the graded choices are correct)
- **Why it's imprecise:** The appoggiatura is the prototypical **accented** (on-beat) NCT, but the **escape tone (échappée) is characteristically UNACCENTED** — it occurs on a weak beat/weak part of the beat. The curriculum text itself says so (`l4u12m2` concept "The Escape Tone": "it lands on a weak beat… a fleeting ornament rather than an expressive accent"). Saying "both are typically accented" is wrong for the escape tone. Graded answers are unaffected, so this is a hint-text nitpick.
- **Correct version:** "Appoggiatura: accented (on-beat). Escape tone: unaccented (weak-beat)."
- **Confidence:** high

### N3. Descending-fifths sequence labeled with a diminished-fifth link without comment
- **Location:** `curriculumL4.ts` `l4u13m5` and `exercisesL4.ts` `l4u13m5e1` / `templatesL4.ts` `l4u13m4`: the diatonic descending-fifths chain "I-IV-vii°-iii-vi-ii-V-I" (C-F-B-E-A-D-G-C).
- **Type:** explanation/exercise
- **Severity:** nitpick
- **Note:** The step **F → B** is a diminished fifth, not a perfect fifth; the diatonic "circle" contains exactly one such irregular link. Standard pedagogy still calls the whole pattern "descending fifths," so this is correct as presented — flagged only because no text acknowledges the single diminished-fifth step, which sharper learners will notice. Optional clarification, not an error.
- **Confidence:** high

## Items explicitly checked and found CORRECT (high confidence)

- **V7 construction (`l4u12m4`, `l4u12m4e1/e2`):** G7 = G-B-D-F; D7 = D-F#-A-C; major triad + minor 7th. Verified against `CHORD_FORMULAS.dominant7 = [0,4,7,10]`.
- **V7 resolution / tritone (`l4u12m4`, `l4u12m4e3`, template `l4u12m4`):** leading tone (3rd of V7) up by half step, chordal 7th down by step; tritone B-F contracts to C-E by contrary motion; resulting I often incomplete (tripled root, no 5th). All correct.
- **V7 inversions (`l4u12m5`, all exercises, template `l4u12m5`):** V6/5 = LT in bass → up to tonic; V4/3 = 5th in bass → I or I6; V4/2 = 7th in bass → must resolve to I6. Figured-bass symbols correct.
- **Diatonic seventh-chord qualities (`l4u13m1`–`m3`):** ii7 = m7 (major key) / iiø7 (minor); iii7, vi7 = m7; Imaj7 = maj7; viiø7 (major) = half-diminished; viio7 (minor) = fully diminished. Verified against `getDiatonicSeventhChords` and `DIATONIC_SEVENTH_CHORD_QUALITIES`.
- **viiø7 as rootless V9 (`l4u13m3`):** Bø7 (B-D-F-A) = 3-5-7-9 of G9 (G-B-D-F-A); resolution B→C, F→E. Correct.
- **Fully-diminished enharmonic symmetry (`l4u13m3`):** equal stacked minor thirds; four enharmonic spellings; pivot-chord use. Correct. `diminished7 = [0,3,6,9]`, letter-distance [0,2,4,6] → B°7 = B-D-F-Ab correctly spelled.
- **Harmonic function categories (`l4u13m4`, exercises, template `l4u13m2`):** T = I, vi, (iii); PD = ii, IV; D = V, vii°. T→PD→D→T normative; ii substitutes for IV; vi for I (deceptive V-vi); vii° for V. Consistent with Kostka/Payne & Laitz. iii classified as (weak) tonic with optional dominant-prep role — defensible.
- **Sequences (`l4u13m5`, exercises, template `l4u13m4`):** descending-fifths = most common; ascending 5-6 = alternating 5/3 and 6/3 ascending by step; descending-thirds shares a common tone per pair; sequences may locally suspend voice-leading prohibitions. All correct.
- **First/second species consonance set (`l4u14m1`, `l4u14m1e1/e2`, template `l4u14m1`):** perfect consonances P1/P5/P8; imperfect m3/M3/m6/M6; **P4 dissonant in two-voice texture**; begin/end on perfect consonance; avoid parallel perfects. Correct (Fux convention).
- **7-8 retardation (`l4u12m2`, `l4u12m2e3`):** leading tone held over V→I, resolves up to tonic; 7th-above-bass → octave. Correct.
- **Minor-key palette (`l4u14m5`, exercises):** V and vii° from harmonic minor; i/iv/VI/III from natural minor; ii/IV from melodic-minor-ascending; III+ (augmented) on degree 3 of harmonic minor; subtonic VII (major) vs leading-tone vii° distinction. Verified against `SCALE_FORMULAS.harmonic_minor` and `DIATONIC_QUALITIES`. Correct.
- **Asymmetric/additive meters (`l4u14m2`, `l4u14m2e1/e2`):** 5 = 2+3 / 3+2; 7 = 2+2+3 / 2+3+2 / 3+2+2; additive groupings. Correct.
- **Chromatic NCTs (`l4u14m3`, exercises):** chromatic passing tone fills a whole step by semitone; chromatic neighbor returns by semitone; embellishment vs structural chromaticism distinction. Correct.
- **Roman-numeral / figured-bass mechanics (`l4u14m4`, exercises):** uppercase = major / lowercase = minor / ° dim / + aug; figures = intervals above bass; F-A-C in C major = IV; C/E = I6 not an E chord. Correct.
- **Enharmonic spelling of generated chord builds:** spot-checked Bb7 (Bb-D-F-Ab), Dm7 (D-F-A-C), Dø7 (D-F-Ab-C), B°7 (B-D-F-Ab), Bø7 (B-D-F-A) — all spelled correctly by `buildChord` via `CHORD_LETTER_DISTANCES`.

## Coverage & limitations

- **What was covered:** All 15 L4 modules' explanatory text; all 45 hand-authored exercises (prompts, choices, correct flags, hints); all 15 template configs (every `choiceSet`, `chordQualities`/`roots`/`accidentals`/`intervals` pool) checked for whether the keyed answer holds across **every** parameter value. Generator semantics traced in `exerciseGenerator.ts` to confirm how templates expand. PT/ES overlays scanned for propagation of the E1 error.
- **Template answer-rule verification:** For each `chord_build` template I confirmed the prompt is quality-agnostic to the root (no key constraint), so every root/accidental value yields a correct, well-spelled chord — no template can emit a wrong chord-build answer. For `multiple_choice` templates, each `choiceSet` has exactly one `correct: true` whose proposition I checked against canon. The single `interval_id` template cannot mis-key an answer (engine-computed) — see Q2 for its framing issue.
- **Not executed:** I did not run the generator or the app; findings are by static reading. I did not audit audio playback, `tryThisQuery` string resolution against the query engine, or non-L4 levels. I did not re-verify the shared `chords.ts`/`intervals.ts`/`scales.ts` formula tables beyond the entries L4 depends on (those formula tables appear correct for the qualities used here).
- **Convention dependence:** Q1 (second-species neighbor dissonance) is the one genuinely convention-dependent call; it is flagged, not asserted as a hard error. The functional classification of iii follows Kostka/Laitz; a strict Riemann-function reading would differ in vocabulary but not in the app's tonic-leaning treatment.
- **Confidence on the two structural errors (E1, E2):** high — both are internal contradictions within the repo (template vs curriculum for E1; template `moduleId` vs module title for E2), independently verifiable without appeal to outside authority.
